In the Peninsula Urban Gardens Society (PUGS), gardening is based on organic gardening principles (see the Code of Ethics below). No chemicals or toxic substances may be used on the gardening sites, and any structures built by individual gardeners must have prior approval from the site coordinator or planning committee.

Due to the high demand for garden plots, each group or family may have only one plot. Gardeners agree to cultivate and weed their own plots throughout the gardening season.

Membership Fees and Plot Deadlines

The membership fee is $30 per year, payable if a garden plot is available. Fees are due on or before April 1. Because the growing season is short, gardeners must begin working their plots by June 1.

If a plot is not in use by June 1, the membership fee will be refunded and the plot will be reassigned to someone on the waiting list.

Communication

Email is the primary means of communication for PUGS. Members share email addresses and agree not to disclose these to third parties or use them for anything other than PUGS business.

Participation and Responsibilities

Because PUGS is run cooperatively, gardeners agree to participate in regular meetings during the gardening season. Beyond tending their own plots, gardeners also volunteer time toward the upkeep and operation of the garden as a whole.

Examples of volunteer activities include:

  • Mowing grass
  • Caring for the berm and herb garden
  • Serving on the Board
  • Building or maintaining garden plots

All gardeners agree to be willing to serve on the Board if needed. Since the gardens are on public property, all members must help keep the site looking neat and attractive.

Gardeners must follow Health Canada's Garden Safety guidelines.

Consensus Management

At the garden-site level, PUGS uses a consensus-based management system. Decisions are made collaboratively, with discussion continuing until all viewpoints have been considered and the group reaches general agreement.

Unlike majority voting, where 51% agreement is enough, consensus aims for near-complete agreement—ideally close to 100%.

How Consensus Works

A problem is presented, and all gardeners contribute ideas and perspectives. Even if viewpoints seem contradictory at first, continued discussion encourages deeper understanding, leading to solutions that integrate everyone's needs.

Examples
Trellis Shade Issue:

Several options were considered - partial trellises, placement at plot ends, or limiting height. Ultimately, gardeners must be considerate of neighbours, and should speak to one another directly if shading becomes a problem.

Gorsebrook Expansion:

Extensive group discussion led to a well-developed plan that accounted for all contingencies. As a result, implementation proceeded smoothly and successfully.

Many visitors note how well-organized Gorsebrook is, and the success of consensus management is often cited as the key factor. It fosters cooperation, trust, and mutual respect—allowing gardeners to combine their strengths to reach shared goals.

Code of Ethics

Gardeners agree to abide by the Peninsula Urban Gardens Society Code of Ethics:

  1. We are a non-profit organization that promotes sustainable urban agriculture.
  2. We use organic gardening practices (no chemical fertilizers, pesticides, herbicides, or treated wood).
  3. We do not plant treated or genetically modified seed.
  4. We treat each other respectfully and interact respectfully with the community (abusive language is not permitted).
  5. We are helpful to fellow gardeners and promote a healthy sense of community.

Liability

PUGS is not responsible for loss of property, personal injury, or damage arising from the use of the community garden. In accordance with Halifax Regional Municipality regulations, gardeners participate at their own risk and must sign a Waiver of Liability.
