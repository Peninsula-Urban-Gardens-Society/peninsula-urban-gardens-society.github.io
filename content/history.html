History of the Peninsula Urban Gardens Society

2011

Following a period of expansion, the two gardening sites—Gorsebrook and Prescott— each became independent registered non-profit organizations. The Prescott site became the Prescott Community Gardens, and the Gorsebrook site became PUGS (the Peninsula Urban Gardens Society).

2008

The organization was renamed the Peninsula Urban Gardens Society to reflect the locations of the Gorsebrook and Prescott gardening sites situated on the Halifax peninsula.

2003

  • Establishment of the Gorsebrook Community Gardens in the South End of Halifax.
  • Continuation of the Home Garden Project, with gardens established at ten additional homes and organizations in HRM.

2002

  • Launch of the Home Garden Project. Four homes in HRM participated in the initial stage.

2001

  • Official opening of the Prescott Street Garden site.
  • Partnership formed with Halifax Adult Services Centre.
  • Water supply established.
  • Tomatoes grown for Plant Gene Resources Canada (PGRC).

2000

  • Two new sets of terraces excavated and constructed on the original Brunswick Street site.
  • Water supply connection installed.
  • Tree and shrub planting throughout the site.
  • Two students hired for a two-month work term through the Nova Scotia Youth Conservation Corps.
  • Youth Corps workers hosted gardening workshops with St. George's YouthNet and the George Dixon Centre day camps.

1999

  • Gardeners expanded and built a third terrace on the original Brunswick Street site with help from the North End Youth Zone.
  • DalTech's Faculty of Architecture began construction of a 3000-liter cistern and rainfall catchment system.
  • Formation of the North End Community Gardening Association (NECGA) in December and incorporation under the Nova Scotia Societies Act.

1998

  • HRM approved construction of a community garden in Murray Warrington Park (Brunswick & Gerrish Streets).
  • Community members formed the Community Garden Task Force.
  • The Task Force designed and built two garden terraces, which were farmed throughout the summer by local gardeners.

Foundation

The North End Community Gardening Association (NECGA) was originally founded in 1999 as a registered non-profit organization to promote urban agriculture and community development.
